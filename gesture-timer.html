Why draw in short, timed poses

Gesture drawing is fast drawing of a pose, figure or object, done against the clock. The time limit matters. In thirty seconds you cannot copy contours or detail, so you have to work out what the pose is doing: the line of action through it, the rhythm of its curves, the tilt of head, ribcage and pelvis, and where the weight falls. Frequent short poses train you to see the whole before the parts. Longer drawings built on that foundation stay lively instead of stiff.

The red curve is the line of action: one continuous line from the head, through the spine, to the leg that carries the weight. In a 30-second pose it may be most of the drawing.
    • Line of actionThe dominant curve or straight line through the pose. Put it down first, in one stroke, and make it more curved than it looks, not less.
    • RhythmContours alternate: a full convex curve on one side of a limb is answered by a flatter or straighter one on the other, and staggered across it. Following that alternation moves the eye through the figure.
    • Weight and balanceFind the supporting leg. A plumb line from the pit of the neck falls on or near it. In contrapposto the pelvis tilts one way and the shoulders the other.
    • Three massesTreat the head, ribcage and pelvis as simple volumes. Their tilt and twist against each other carry most of the movement.
    • EconomyDraw from the shoulder with a soft medium or the side of the lead, on cheap paper such as newsprint. Accept distortion. A short pose records what you saw, not a finished image.

      Good sources: your own photos of friends posing, pose-reference sites, or stills from dance and sport. If the session has more poses than images, the images repeat.

      Sample figures

      A new mannequin pose is generated for every pose in the session: standing, lunging, reaching, crouching, running, throwing and kneeling, each varied at random and sometimes mirrored. Mannequins are simplified, so use them to practise line of action and the three masses, then move to photographs or life.

      For drawing from life with no reference images: each pose shows one thing to find in the room (or yourself, in a mirror) and draw. Objects have gesture too: the thrust of a chair leg, the slump of a bag, the curve of a draped cloth.
