Ellipses in perspective

Any circle that is not square-on to you appears as an ellipse: the rims of cups, wheels, bottle tops, columns and bowls. Learn how eye level controls the shape of an ellipse, how it lines up with an object's axis, the classic mistakes, and how to construct and practise them.

Key terms

  • EllipseA circle seen at an angle. It is a smooth, symmetrical oval with rounded ends, never pointed and never flat-sided.
  • Major axisThe longest line across the ellipse, through its centre. It shows the true width of the circle and does not foreshorten.
  • Minor axisThe shortest line across, at exactly 90° to the major axis. It is the foreshortened depth of the circle and always lies along the object's central axis.
  • Ellipse degreeThe angle at which you see the circle. 0° is edge-on, a straight line; 90° is face-on, a full circle. Most everyday ellipses are between 10° and 60°.
  • Eye level (horizon)A circle level with your eye is seen edge-on as a line. Horizontal circles open up the further they sit above or below it.
  • Central axisThe imaginary line through the centre of a round object, such as the spindle of a cylinder or the axle of a wheel. Every ellipse on the object is built around it.

Measuring the degree: minor axis ÷ major axis = sin(degree). A 30° ellipse is half as tall as it is wide (sin 30° = 0.5); a 45° ellipse is about 0.71 as tall; a 10° ellipse only about 0.17.

Tilted axis

Tip a cylinder over and its ellipses tip with it. Whatever the angle, the minor axis of each end lies along the cylinder's central axis and the major axis crosses it at a right angle. Drag on the drawing to rotate the cylinder, or use the sliders.

The more the axis points towards you, the rounder the ends. The far end is always a little rounder or narrower than the near one because it is seen from a slightly different angle. In strong perspective the alignment is very slightly off; in practice, draw the minor axis on the central axis.

Guess the degree

Judge the degree of each ellipse by eye, then check. Compare the minor axis with the major axis: half as tall is 30°, about three-quarters is 50°.

Practice sheet

An A4 sheet with a reference row of ellipse degrees from 10° to 80° and rows of boxes to fill with freehand ellipses. Each box is exactly the right proportion for its degree, so a good ellipse touches the middle of all four sides.

ELLIPSE PRACTICE

Freehand ellipses: match each row's degree and touch every side of the box.

NAME DATE
  1. Ghost the movement first: circle the pencil above the paper a few times from the elbow or shoulder, then lower it and draw lightly in two or three passes.
  2. Touch the midpoint of every side of the box. The ends must be round, not pointed, and the far half the same shape as the near half.
  3. Check your degree against the reference row: minor axis ÷ major axis = sin(degree).
