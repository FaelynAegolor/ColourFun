Step 1 of 7

Shading with a pencil

Every shaded shape has the same parts of light and shadow. Learn their names, then use a value scale to match your pencil pressure to them.

Value scale

  1. 1 · Paper whiteHighlights. Don't shade at all.
  2. 2 · LightFaces the light. 2H or H, very gentle.
  3. 3 · Mid-toneTurning away. HB, even pressure.
  4. 4 · DarkIn shadow. 2B, layers of hatching.
  5. 5 · DarkestCore and cast shadow. 4B–6B.

H pencils are hard and light. B pencils are soft and dark: the bigger the number, the darker. HB is in the middle.

  • Start lightSketch with a 2H or HB and hardly any pressure. You can always go darker, but it's hard to go lighter.
  • One light sourceDecide where the light comes from before you shade, and keep it the same for the whole drawing.
  • Build in layersGet dark areas by going over them a few times, not by pressing really hard straight away.
  • Follow the formStraight strokes for flat faces, curved strokes that wrap around round shapes.
  • Save the whiteLeave the paper white for highlights, or lift them back out with a putty rubber.
  • SquintSquinting hides the detail so you can see if your lights and darks are right.
  • Don't smudgeRest your hand on a scrap of paper. Blend with a tissue or blending stump, not your finger.
  • Edges meetWhere a light face meets a dark face, make the dark side darkest right at the edge. It makes the corner pop.
