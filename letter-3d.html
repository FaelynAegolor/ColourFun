3D letter in one-point perspective

Turn a flat block letter into a solid 3D letter using one vanishing point, at least 2 cm thick, shaded with the light coming from the right. Pick a letter, move the vanishing point, follow the six steps, then print it at true size.

  • The brief
  • Letter builder
  • Shading
  • Tips

The brief

Submit

  • Your letter in 3D, one-point perspective.
  • A minimum thickness of 2 cm (the depth you see on the top).
  • Shading, with the light source coming from the right.

The method (as in the class video)

  1. Draw the letter flat with a ruler.
  2. Place one vanishing point.
  3. Join every corner to the vanishing point.
  4. Cut off the thickness and draw the back edges parallel to the front.
  5. Rub out the guide lines and hidden edges.
  6. Shade each face for the light from the right.

Shading with the light from the right

Every face gets one tone, set by which way it faces. The light comes from the right (and a little from above), so the tones run from light on the right-facing faces to darkest on the left-facing ones. Keep each face an even tone and make neighbouring faces clearly different.

Faces turned rightLightest: they face the light.
Top facesLight grey.
Bottom facesMid to dark grey.
Faces turned leftDarkest: they face away from the light.
Front faceLeave it white, or use one flat colour.

Tips

  • Everything goes to one pointEvery edge that goes back into the page must line up with the vanishing point. Use a ruler every time.
  • Back edges are parallelThe back edges are parallel to the front edges: horizontals stay horizontal, verticals stay vertical, diagonals keep the same angle.
  • Where to put the VPAbove the letter shows the top faces (the thickness the brief asks for). Off to one side shows that side as well.
  • Check the 2 cmMeasure the shortest edge going back to the VP. Lines further from the VP are longer, so if the shortest is 2 cm, they all are.
  • Clean before you shadeRub out the guide lines and the hidden back edges first, then go over the visible edges firmly.
  • Even tonesShade each face with the side of the pencil in one direction, building up layers rather than pressing hard.
