Paint mixing

Paint is not light

Screens mix colour additively: red, green and blue light add together towards white. Paint mixes subtractively: each pigment absorbs part of the spectrum, and a mixture reflects only what survives every pigment in it, so mixes move towards dark and grey.

A pigment particle both absorbs and scatters light. Kubelka–Munk theory describes an opaque paint film by the ratio of the two, K/S, at each wavelength. Mixing paints averages their K and S, not their screen colours, which is why blue and yellow paint make green: yellow absorbs blue light, blue absorbs red and yellow light, and the band that both reflect, green, survives.

Averaging the RGB values of the same two colours (see the four swatches) gives a pale, greyed colour, because averaging light cannot remove the red and blue components the way a pigment does. The simulated paint mix is darker and much greener.

Every simulation on this page applies Kubelka–Munk per sRGB channel, weighted by parts and by an estimate of each pigment's tinting strength. Real pigments have full spectral curves, so treat the results as a guide to direction and proportion, not a colour-accurate prediction: brands differ, and a screen cannot show paint's gloss, texture or transparency.

        Warm and cool primaries

        No tube is a pure primary. Every yellow, red and blue leans towards one of its neighbours on the wheel, so painters keep a split-primary palette: a warm and a cool version of each. Choose the pair that leans towards each other for a clean secondary, and the pair that leans away for a deliberately muted one.

        • Clean mixes: Cadmium Yellow Light and Phthalo Blue both reflect strongly in the green part of the spectrum, so their overlap is a strong green.
        • Muddy mixes: Cadmium Yellow leans to orange and Ultramarine leans to violet. Each carries a trace of red, green's complement, so the mixture contains all three primaries and greys down.
        • Use both deliberately: landscape greens often need the muted pair, or a clean green knocked back with a touch of red. Mud is only a problem when it is unintended.
